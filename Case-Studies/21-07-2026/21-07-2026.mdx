# Silent Account Tampering and PII Exposure in a National Political Party Management System

## Executive Summary

A routine interaction with a national political party registration platform surfaced an unauthenticated password-tampering flaw during ordinary use — not a targeted hunt. That accidental discovery led to a scoped, self-initiated review of the platform's authentication, session, and membership-management surfaces, using researcher-owned test accounts only. The engagement confirmed six issues spanning two High and three Medium severities: silent, unauthenticated credential tampering on any account; exposure of sensitive personal data — including special-category fields — through identifier-based lookups; unauthorized modification of a user's political party registration; and a session-invalidation gap. One area of the platform, by contrast, was found to be a strong model of correct design. Findings were reported through a coordinated disclosure channel; this case study reflects the assessment as submitted, pending acknowledgment and remediation.

## Engagement Scope and Methodology

### Target Overview

The target is a web-based platform used to manage national political party membership records — registration, membership status lookups, and party-affiliation changes — serving a public-sector regulatory function. <RedactedPath domain path="/api/v2" visible="UserSignin" /> and related authentication and membership-management endpoints made up the primary surface reviewed.

### Methodology

Manual, black-box testing was performed exclusively against researcher-owned test accounts. The approach covered: authentication and session-lifecycle behavior, parameter-manipulation testing against identifier-bound endpoints, and error-handling review under malformed input. No automated scanning, fuzzing, or denial-of-service technique was used against the application. Personally identifiable information belonging to real account holders was accessed only to the minimum extent necessary to confirm that an access-control failure existed, and was not retained, exported, or shared beyond what coordinated disclosure requires.

## Findings

### Silent, Unauthenticated Credential Tampering

#### Description
The password-change flow allowed a password to be set on an account identified only by its public identifier, with no verification that the requester was the account owner.

#### Technical Detail
The endpoint responsible for validating a user's current password before allowing a reset did not meaningfully check the submitted value against the target account, and the subsequent change request completed using an attacker-supplied account identifier rather than a value bound to an authenticated session. This was demonstrated end-to-end between two researcher-controlled accounts, including against an account originally provisioned through single sign-on, confirming the new credential became usable at login regardless of the account's original authentication method. No notification was generated for the affected account on either request.

#### Business Impact
An attacker with only a target's public identifier — not a secret — can silently plant a working password on that account. A second factor currently prevents this from becoming full takeover on its own, but it establishes a standing, invisible foothold on any account in the system, with no signal to the legitimate owner that anything changed.

### Sensitive Personal Data Exposure via Identifier-Based Lookup

#### Description
Two related endpoints returned detailed personal records — including special-category data — for any valid identifier, without confirming the requester's authorization to view that record.

#### Technical Detail
Supplying a valid public identifier to either endpoint returned the associated individual's contact details, date of birth, and demographic fields including ethnicity and religion, along with party-membership data, independent of the requester's own session or identity.

#### Business Impact
This converts a single valid identifier into a full personal-profile lookup, at a scale limited only by how quickly identifiers can be supplied. For a platform handling politically sensitive affiliation data, the combined exposure of contact information, demographic data, and party membership carries meaningful privacy and regulatory weight.

### Unauthorized Modification of Party Registration

#### Description
A membership-update endpoint accepted a target account identifier from the client and processed a party-affiliation change against it, while correctly rejecting changes to that same account's stored contact fields.

#### Technical Detail
The inconsistency was the key signal: contact-field changes submitted for a non-owned account were silently ignored server-side, while the party-affiliation portion of the same request was accepted and applied — confirming partial, not full, request validation.

#### Business Impact
A user's political party registration is a sensitive, publicly meaningful status. That it can be altered by a third party without consent or notification is a business-logic failure with real integrity implications for a system of public record.

<RedactedPath domain path="/api/v2/PartyMembershipStatus" visible="UpdateMembershipDetails" />

### Session Not Invalidated on Logout

#### Description
A session token captured before logout continued to authenticate successfully after the logout flow had completed.

#### Technical Detail
Logout appeared to clear the session token client-side only; the token itself remained valid server-side, and its validity window was observed to extend further upon any subsequent authenticated request made with it.

#### Business Impact
A captured or retained session token has a effectively unbounded lifetime as long as it continues to be used, undermining the security value of a user-initiated logout.

#### Scope Note
Session/cookie handling, platform-wide.

---

### Verbose Error Disclosure

#### Description
Malformed input to several endpoints returned full framework stack traces rather than a handled error response.

#### Technical Detail
Disclosed detail included internal file-system paths, framework and runtime version information, and internal class/method names — useful reconnaissance material for an attacker studying the platform, though not independently exploited here.

#### Business Impact
Low direct impact, but it measurably lowers the effort required for further attack surface mapping.

#### Scope Note
Multiple endpoints under malformed or missing parameters.

## Architecture Intelligence (Recon Output)

Passive reconnaissance of the platform's broader network neighborhood identified additional internet-facing services beyond the application itself, including what appeared to be network management interfaces on perimeter equipment. These were not tested or interacted with beyond passive identification, fall outside this engagement's application-layer scope, and were relayed to the operator separately as a hardening observation rather than a tested finding. No specific host, service, or configuration detail is reproduced here, consistent with this engagement's handling of infrastructure-level observations.

## Observations on Program Quality

The platform showed a clear split between well-designed and under-validated flows built by, it appears, different levels of care. The party-resignation workflow — requiring member-existence verification, registration-state checks, and OTP/session validation at every step before completing an irreversible action — stands out as a strong template. The same rigor was not consistently extended to the password-reset and membership-update flows, despite handling comparably sensitive operations. This is a common pattern in fast-shipped government and civic-tech platforms: security-critical flows added or modified later in a project's life don't always inherit the review standard of the original design.

There was, at the time of writing, no clearly published vulnerability disclosure contact or policy for this platform — reports were routed to a general institutional contact address, which is consistent with a broader pattern seen across public-sector platforms in this market: the technical capability to build these systems exists, but the process for someone to responsibly report a problem after launch generally does not.

## Recommendations

- Bind account-modifying operations (password changes, membership updates) to the authenticated session's own identity rather than a client-supplied identifier.
- Extend the validation discipline already present on contact-field protection to every field accepted by the same endpoint.
- Invalidate session tokens server-side on logout rather than relying on client-side clearing.
- Treat demographic fields such as ethnicity and religion as requiring explicit authorization to retrieve, separate from general membership-status data.
- Disable verbose error output in production and handle malformed input gracefully within application code.
- Publish a clear, monitored vulnerability disclosure contact — the single highest-leverage, lowest-cost change available, given how much of this engagement's friction was about finding a channel rather than finding the bugs.
